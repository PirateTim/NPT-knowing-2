#### 5.9 The Honeypot (The Traitorous API)
While the State was hiding its own identity, it was purchasing yours. But the State wasn't the only buyer. The myth of the "Twitter Revolution" was that the Open Web was a Library—a safe space to read and write. The reality was that the Open Web was a Registry. The betrayal was structural. It wasn't just spies lurking in chat rooms; it was the API (Application Programming Interface) itself. The platforms didn't just leak data; they productized it.

The first buyer was the State. In 2016, the ACLU exposed Geofeedia, a data broker that purchased "Firehose" access from Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram. Geofeedia marketed this access to over 500 law enforcement agencies, explicitly touting its ability to track #BlackLivesMatter protests (ACLU, 2016). The "Distributed Audit" (protesters documenting police misconduct) was captured, packaged, and sold back to the police as a "Target Package". The tool of liberation became the tool of the dragnet.

The second buyer was the Psychographic Manipulator. If Geofeedia weaponized the Public Feed, Cambridge Analytica (2015) weaponized the Social Graph. The mechanism was the "Open Graph" API. Facebook allowed an innocuous app ("This Is Your Digital Life") to harvest data not just from the users who took the quiz, but from their friends (Cadwalladr & Graham-Harrison, 2018). This harvest of 87 million profiles destroyed the concept of "Consent" in the Knowledge System. You could be profiled, targeted, and manipulated not because you clicked a link, but because your friend did. The "Social Graph"—the map of human trust—became a weapon of Information Warfare.

This surveillance didn't stay at the federal or corporate level. It trickled down to the Municipal Stalker. In 2021, Los Angeles Sheriff Alex Villanueva used social media monitoring tools to obsessively track his critics, specifically targeting journalist Cerise Castle after she exposed the "Banditos" deputy gang. He escalated his war on knowledge in April 2022 by announcing a criminal leak investigation into LA Times reporter Alene Tchekmedyian for the crime of publishing a video of deputy abuse (Knoll, 2022). The "Open Web" had become a trap. By equating "Public Speech" with "Consent to Surveillance," we allowed the State and the Corporation to punish the act of knowing.
