#### 4.22 The Keepers of the Secondary Elaborations
Why do they do it? Why does a highly credentialed journalist sit in a demo, watch an AI invent a legal case or a historical fact, and write a headline about "The Future of Law" instead of "The Automated Perjury Machine"? It is not simply corruption. To understand this behavior, we must look not to economics, but to the anthropology of magic.

In his seminal 1937 work Witchcraft, Oracles and Magic among the Azande, anthropologist E.E. Evans-Pritchard described how the Azande people maintained their belief in the "Poison Oracle" (a ritual where a chicken was fed poison to determine the truth) even when it failed or contradicted itself. When the Oracle was wrong, the tribe did not conclude that the magic was fake. They employed what Evans-Pritchard called "Secondary Elaborations of Belief" (Evans-Pritchard, 1937). They claimed the poison was stale, or the ritual was interrupted, or that bad spirits had interfered. The excuse protected the system.

The modern tech press functions as the Priestly Class of the Silicon Valley Oracle. Their role is not to verify the magic, but to generate the Secondary Elaborations required to excuse its failure. When the AI lies, they do not say, "The Intelligence is fake." They say, "It is a beta." They say, "It is a research preview." They say, "You prompted it wrong." As journalist Dan Lyons observed of his time inside the bubble, "The tech press is not journalism. It is the PR wing of the Venture Capital industry" (Lyons, 2016). They are the keepers of the excuse, rationalizing the hallucination so the stock price—and the Spectacle—can remain intact.
