#### 4.31 The Nature of the Sting
We return, finally, to the fable of the Scorpion and the Frog. In the standard retelling, the Scorpion stings the Frog halfway across the river, dooming them both, and offers the famous justification: "It is my nature." For decades, media critics have attempted to analyze this sting through the lens of economics. They argued that the Scorpion stung the truth because it was profitable—that the sensationalism of the lie sold more papers than the boredom of the fact. But this diagnosis grants the Scorpion a rationality it does not possess. The Scorpion did not sting the Frog to secure a Q4 book deal or to boost quarterly engagement metrics. He stung the Frog because that is what he does.

The modern press has evolved into an entity that is ontologically incapable of transporting a "Ground Truth" across the river of history without dissolving it. The "First Draft of History" is no longer a preservation of reality; it is the venom that breaks it down. The industry has replaced the Documentation of the Act with the Coverage of the Allegation. It does not matter if the Frog is "Truth" or "Democracy" or "Public Health"; the Scorpion must inject "Context," "Counter-Narratives," and "Both-Sides" framing until the original object is unrecognizable. The sting is not a transaction; it is a reflex.
