#### 4.4 Part II: The Industrialization of Nihilism
Whenever the modern press is confronted with its own decay, it retreats to a standard, reflexive defense: "It has always been this way." They point to the Yellow Journalism of the 1890s, to William Randolph Hearst inciting the Spanish-American War, and argue that the current crisis is merely a new coat of paint on an old vice (Swanberg, 1961).

William Randolph Hearst was a monster, but he was a monster with Agency. When he famously cabled his illustrator in Cuba, "You furnish the pictures and I'll furnish the war," he was acting with Malice (Swanberg, 1961). He intended to shape the world to his specific, imperial desires. He believed in something, even if that something was conquest. The pathology of the 21st-century press is not Malice; it is Indifference.

The modern editor does not want to start a war; he does not want to stop a war; he simply wants to remain "in the room" to cover the debate about the war. As Jon Stewart diagnosed in a confrontation with Chris Wallace in 2011, the bias of the media is not Liberal or Conservative. "The bias of the mainstream media is toward sensationalism, conflict, and laziness" (Stewart, 2011). But this is not the laziness of the sluggard; it is Militant Laziness. It is an ideological commitment to superficiality, pursued with a zeal that rivals any religious fundamentalism. It is a refusal to look out the window because opening the blinds might alienate a subscriber.
