### 10.12 The Screen Defense: The Technodeterminism Lie

Defenders of the system often revert to a lazy historical alibi: **Technodeterminism.**

*"It's not the curriculum,"* they argue. *"It's the phones. The students are stupid because of TikTok and the iPhone."*

This is a falsehood. It is a convenient lie used to absolve the policy by blaming the device. We must reject it. The timeline of the "Empty Vessel" correlates perfectly with the device, but the causality is reversed. The Common Core (2010) and the iPad (2010) arrived simultaneously.

The phone brought the virus—distraction, fragmentation, dopamine loops—but the **Curriculum removed the antibodies.**

A mind fortified with deep historical schemas, formal logic, and the stamina developed by reading complex, book-length narratives might have withstood the fracture of the screen. A student who had read *The Power Broker* or *Moby Dick* would possess the "Attention Span" to resist the 15-second clip. Instead, the school system **capitulated**.

The curriculum mimicked the device. It replaced books with "excerpts." It replaced essays with "snippet-based evidence." It replaced sustained thought with "just-in-time" retrieval. The student didn't become a "Surface Skimmer" because of Instagram; they became a Surface Skimmer because the State of New York mandated it. To blame the technology is to confuse the weapon with the wound.
