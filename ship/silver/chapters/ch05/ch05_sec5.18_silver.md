#### 5.18 The Requiem (The Diaspora)
The "Sensible Exodus" that began as a retreat for safety became a forced evacuation. But unlike the migrations of the past, there was no promised land. The refugees of the Trust Graph didn't build a new city; they scattered into the wilderness.

We lost the Archive. The Library of Congress, which had once ambitiously planned to archive every Tweet, gave up the project in 2017, overwhelmed by the volume [Loc.gov. (2017). Update on the Twitter Archive. Library of Congress., URL: https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2017/12/update-on-the-twitter-archive/]. When Musk purged the servers and users deleted their accounts in protest, the primary source documents of the early 21st century vanished. We erased our own history.

We lost the Network. Some went to Mastodon, which felt like returning to Linux—technically pure but sociologically empty. Some went to Bluesky, trying to rebuild the chronological protocol. Millions went to Threads, Meta’s answer to Twitter. But it was a lobotomized square. Instagram CEO Adam Mosseri explicitly stated that Threads would not "encourage" politics or hard news, arguing that the engagement wasn't worth the scrutiny [Mosseri. (2023). Statement on Politics. Threads., URL: https://www.threads.net/@mosseri/post/CzJ0sH2rR5k]. It was a platform designed for "Brand Safety," not human reality.

The ultimate cost was the loss of Global Search. In the Twitter era, you could search a keyword and see the world unfolding. Now, we inhabit Feudalism. You only know what happens in your specific Discord server, your specific Substack, or your specific group chat. The "Mass Media" age ended, and the "Micro-Reality" age began. We are no longer "online" together. We are just alone, in different rooms, staring at different walls.
