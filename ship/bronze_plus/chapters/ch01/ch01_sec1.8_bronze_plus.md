#### 1.8 Knowing
The intellectual validation of the "End of Knowing" arrived not through a single discovery, but through the convergence of two long-standing academic adversaries. To understand why the "Camp Mystic" lie was structurally inevitable, we must look to the spring of 2023, when the two opposing poles of cognitive science—Noam Chomsky and Yann LeCun—abruptly aligned to attack a common threat.

The opening salvo came on March 8, 2023. Noam Chomsky, the father of modern linguistics, published a guest essay in The New York Times titled "The False Promise of ChatGPT". Writing with Ian Roberts and Jeffrey Watumull, Chomsky did not classify the new chatbots as "intelligence," but as "high-tech plagiarism" and a "marvel of description" devoid of explanation. Drawing on his theory of Universal Grammar, he argued that the machine operated on a "pre-human" cognitive level. It could describe what is (based on statistical frequency), but it was structurally incapable of understanding what isn't—the counterfactuals and physical impossibilities that define human thought. He concluded that the machine’s amoral fluidity was a defect, stating, "ChatGPT exhibits something like the banality of evil: plagiarism and apathy and obviation" [Chomsky, Noam, Roberts, Ian, & Watumull, Jeffrey (2023), "The False Promise of ChatGPT", The New York Times, March 8, 2023, URL: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/03/08/opinion/noam-chomsky-chatgpt-ai.html].

In the normal course of the "AI Wars," Yann LeCun—the Chief AI Scientist at Meta and a pioneer of Deep Learning—would have been Chomsky’s fiercest critic. For decades, LeCun has championed "Connectionism" (the idea that machines learn from data), explicitly rejecting Chomsky’s theory of "Innateness" (the idea that grammar is hard-wired). Yet, following the Op-Ed, LeCun did not counter-attack. He validated the diagnosis. On social media and in subsequent lectures throughout 2024, LeCun effectively opened a second front against the specific architecture of Large Language Models (LLMs). While he disagreed with Chomsky on how biological brains learn, he agreed that these silicon brains were broken.

He famously declared that "Auto-Regressive LLMs are doomed" and warned the industry that "on the highway toward human-level AI, a large language model is basically an off-ramp — a distraction, a dead end" [LeCun, Yann (2024), "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence: Why Auto-Regressive Generative AI Is an Off-Ramp", Meta AI Keynotes & Technical Whitepapers, URL: https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-world-models/]. LeCun’s critique was architectural. He argued that because these models operate solely on Autoregression (predicting the next word), they lack a "World Model"—an internal simulation of cause and effect [LeCun, Yann (2024), "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence: Why Auto-Regressive Generative AI Is an Off-Ramp", Meta AI Keynotes & Technical Whitepapers, URL: https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-world-models/]. They know that the words "dropped glass" are statistically attracted to the word "shatter," but they do not know why the glass shatters. They have no concept of gravity, mass, or time. LeCun pointed out the staggering inefficiency of this approach: a modern LLM is trained on text equivalent to 400,000 years of human reading, yet it still fails at basic physical reasoning tasks that a four-year-old can solve, such as clearing a dinner table [LeCun, Yann (2024), "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence: Why Auto-Regressive Generative AI Is an Off-Ramp", Meta AI Keynotes & Technical Whitepapers, URL: https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-world-models/].

By December 2025, this dialogue had shifted from theoretical debate to forensic reality. The "Camp Mystic" lie was the perfect child of this convergence: it satisfied Chomsky’s rules of grammar (it sounded like a news report) and LeCun’s rules of probability (it predicted the tokens for "rescue"), but it failed the only rule that matters—physical reality.
