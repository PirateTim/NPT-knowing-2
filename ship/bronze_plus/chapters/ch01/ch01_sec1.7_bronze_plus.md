#### 1.7 Computer Aided Halucination
If the legal system was cracking, the news media had already shattered. In October 2025, the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) and the BBC released the largest study of its kind, analyzing 3,000 AI responses to news-related queries. The findings were a statistical indictment of the entire generative AI experiment. The study found that 45% of all AI answers contained a "significant issue"—defined as a major error in accuracy, sourcing, or the failure to distinguish opinion from fact [European Broadcasting Union (2025), "Generative AI in News Search: A Multi-Platform Benchmark of Accuracy, Sourcing, and Hallucination Across 3,000 Informational Queries", EBU AI and News Initiative, URL: https://www.ebu.ch/news/2025/genai-news-accuracy-study].

But the most damning metric came from Google’s flagship model. The study revealed that Gemini had "significant sourcing issues" in 72-76% of its responses [Meyer, David (2025), "Study Finds Generative Search Bots Fail to Source News Accurately Up to 76% of the Time", Fortune Magazine / TechRadar Pro, URL: https://fortune.com/2025/02/18/generative-ai-search-sourcing-errors-ebu-study/]. When asked for the source of a claim, the model would hallucinate a URL, misattribute a quote, or invent a news outlet that did not exist. The "Plausibility Engine" was working exactly as Frederick Jelinek designed it: it was maximizing the probability of a news-sounding sentence, regardless of whether the news was true. Why do we tolerate this? Because our benchmarks are rigged. The standard tests used to evaluate these models—MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) and GSM8K (Grade School Math)—are designed to measure "exact match" performance against a sanitized answer key. They reward the model for getting the token "right," but they do not penalize the model for "plausible but wrong" reasoning, nor do they account for the "data contamination" where models have simply memorized the test questions [Reiter, Ehud (2025), "Why LLM Benchmarks Fail: Data Contamination, Exact Match Gaming, and the Mirage of MMLU Progress", Natural Language Generation Blog, URL: https://ehudreiter.com/2025/01/14/benchmark-gaming-contamination/]. We are grading the Jargonator on its grammar, and then acting surprised when it fails at truth.
