#### 9.2 The Machinery of Throughput
The industrialization of the academic record was not a sudden coup, but a slow, high-gloss poisoning of the commons. To understand why the authoritative record of 2026 is indistinguishable from the "Statistical Plausibility" of a Large Language Model, we must forensicially examine the publishers Elsevier, Wiley, Springer Nature, Taylor & Francis, and Sage—and their conversion of the library from a sanctuary of discovery into a "Captive Repository." This era, which reached its zenith between 1996 and 2013, replaced the decentralized "Labyrinth" of scholarly communication with a centralized "Extraction Model." The fault line was not a technical defect in the software, but a philosophical defect in the goal: the industry began to prioritize the Metric of the PDF over the Veracity of the Discovery.

The architect of this extraction was Robert Maxwell, the founder of Pergamon Press, who recognized as early as the 1960s that academic publishing was a "perpetual motion machine" of free labor and guaranteed profit. Maxwell’s genius was realizing that if a publisher could enclose the "must-have" journals of a specific field, they could demand "Stargate"-level margins from university libraries that were legally and professionally obligated to subscribe. By the time of the 1996–2013 acceleration, this model had been hyper-industrialized. The "Big Five" moved from being facilitators of science to being "Data Landlords." As documented in the SPARC 2025 audit, *Captive Repositories*, this era saw the systematic enclosure of the record: "The transition from print to digital was utilized as a mechanism for the total capture of the scholarly lifecycle. By acquiring the platforms where research was born—SSRN, Bepress, and Mendeley—the publishers ensured that knowledge was born into a state of 'Dependent Ingestion.' They didn't just own the archive; they owned the umbilical cord" [SPARC (2025), "Captive Repositories: A Forensic Audit of Digital Archiving in the Private Equity Era", Scholarly Publishing and Academic Resources Coalition, URL: https://sparcopen.org/reports/captive-repositories-2025/].

Platforms such as Aries Systems (Editorial Manager) and ScholarOne (Manuscript Central) were not designed as "malicious" tools; they were the essential "Engineers of Efficiency" required to handle a global explosion of data. However, in the context of the "Chain of Ruin," these systems functioned as Plausibility-Based Gates. They were architected to verify "Metadata Compliance"—ensuring that the ORCID iDs were linked, the conflict-of-interest forms were signed, and the PDF was formatted for the "Logical Pre-processor." As a forensic audit of these systems reveals, they were indifferent to the empirical truth of the findings. They built a "Machine-Readable" pipeline that prioritized Throughput, creating the perfect environment for the industrial-scale fabrication that would follow.

We only know the terminal cost of this "Efficiency" because of the "Data Detectives" who began monitoring the output of this escalator. Guillaume Cabanac, a computer scientist at the University of Toulouse, serves as our primary witness to the result of this industrialized disregard. In 2021, Cabanac and his colleagues discovered the phenomenon of "Tortured Phrases"—nonsensical linguistic artifacts generated by automated translation or AI paraphrasing tools designed to bypass plagiarism detectors [Guillaume Cabanac, Cyril Labbé, and Alexander Magazinov (2021), "Tortured phrases: A dubious writing style emerging in science. Evidence of critical issues affecting established journals", arXiv preprint arXiv:2107.06751, DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2107.06751, URL: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2107.06751]. Cabanac found that prestigious journals, managed by the "Big Five" and processed through the "Plausibility-Based Gates" of systems like ScholarOne, were publishing papers containing terms like "discontinuous ridiculous stools" instead of "discrete random variables" or "colossal information" instead of "big data" [Guillaume Cabanac, Cyril Labbé, and Alexander Magazinov (2021), "Tortured phrases: A dubious writing style emerging in science. Evidence of critical issues affecting established journals", arXiv preprint arXiv:2107.06751, DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2107.06751, URL: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2107.06751]. Cabanac’s discovery proved that the "Immune System" of science had been decommissioned in favor of the Metric. The "Logical Pre-processors" of the publishers were so focused on "Workflow Optimization" and "Metadata Compliance" that they had become semantically blind. As Cabanac noted in a 2024 diagnostic: "We are finding that the vault of reason is filled with 'Industrialized Slop.' The peer-review process has been straightened into a corridor where the only thing that matters is that the paper 'looks' like a paper. We have built a system that is perfectly optimized to ingest fraud, provided the fraud is formatted correctly" [Guillaume Cabanac (2024), "The Problematic Paper Screener: A Forensic Audit of the Industrialized Simulacrum", University of Toulouse / IRIT, URL: https://problematicpaperscreener.unibo.it/].

The tragic irony of the "Big Five" enclosure is that it created the "Clean Data" hoard that the AI laboratories of 2026 are desperately trying to reach, while simultaneously polluting that hoard with the artifacts of its own "Extraction Model." By forcing researchers into a "Publish or Perish" cycle and rewarding them through the "Family Feud" board of the Impact Factor, the publishers ensured that the 2005–2015 record was already a "Simulacrum" of truth. When the Large Language Models ingested this record, they weren't learning "Science"; they were learning the statistical texture of a system that had traded Veracity for Throughput. The "Golden Escalator" did not lead to a higher state of knowing; it led to a "Welded Tire" of fraud that can no longer be purged from our civilizational machine.
