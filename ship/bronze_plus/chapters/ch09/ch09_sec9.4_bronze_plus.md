#### 9.4 The Fraud and the Forward Compatibility Crisis
The integrity of the academic record rests upon a singular, foundational assumption: that the evidence supporting a claim exists, persists, and remains retrievable for verification. For three centuries, the physical reality of the printed page enforced a rigid coupling between the "Assertion" and the "Ground Truth." However, the transition to digital distribution introduced the "Supplemental Material" link—an innovation that effectively decoupled the "Claim" (the PDF) from the "Proof" (the data). We are now living in the wreckage of that decoupling, a state I call the "Hollow Library." This failure of "Forward Compatibility" is not a technical glitch; it is a dereliction of duty by the "Learned Societies" and a consequence of the aggressive financialization of scholarly infrastructure.

We know the magnitude of this decay through the forensic audit of the 2005–2015 "Lost Decade." Longitudinal studies confirm that the "Supplemental Material" hyperlink is the most fragile component of the scholarly record. In biomedical publications, approximately 40% of links to supplemental materials are broken within just one year of publication. By the fourth year, a third of the scientific record becomes effectively unverifiable. This "Velocity of Rot" proves that for a significant portion of modern science, the evidence does not even survive the initial period of peer review. The "Texture of Truth"—the blue underlined text of a hyperlink—was sufficient to satisfy the gatekeepers, even if the destination was a void.

This systemic failure occurred under the watch of the major Learned Societies—the MLA, the AHA, and the IEEE. As the guardians of their respective disciplines, these organizations bore the responsibility of establishing "Standards for Persistence," yet an analysis of their guidelines reveals a pattern of "Epistemic Procrastination." The Modern Language Association focused on the aesthetics of the citation rather than the persistence of the source [Modern Language Association (MLA) (2015), "Guidelines for Evaluating Work in Digital Humanities and Digital Media", Modern Language Association, URL: https://www.mla.org/About-Us/Governance/Committees/Committee-Listings/Professional-Issues/Committee-on-Information-Technology/Guidelines-for-Evaluating-Work-in-Digital-Humanities-and-Digital-Media]. The American Historical Association obsessed over legitimizing digital formats as a vehicle for historical "argument" while ignoring the fragility of the digital "evidence" [American Historical Association (AHA) (2015), "Guidelines for the Evaluation of Digital Scholarship in History", American Historical Association, URL: https://www.historians.org/teaching-and-learning/digital-history-resources/guidelines-for-the-evaluation-of-digital-scholarship-in-history]. Even the IEEE, which attempted more proactive solutions like "DataPort," initially classified supplemental code and datasets as "nonessential," relegating the "receipts" of machine learning and signal processing to volatile, non-archival author websites [IEEE (2023), "IEEE DataPort Guidelines for Archival Research Datasets and Supplemental Code Repositories", Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, URL: https://ieee-dataport.org/about-ieee-dataport].

The final enclosure of this "Hollow Library" was executed by private equity. The defining event was the capture of repositories by commercial giants like Elsevier. In May 2016, Elsevier acquired the Social Science Research Network (SSRN), and in August 2017, they acquired Bepress, the developer of Digital Commons [Elsevier (2016), "Elsevier Acquires Social Science Research Network (SSRN)", Elsevier Press Releases, URL: https://www.elsevier.com/about/press-releases/corporate/elsevier-acquires-ssrn] [Elsevier (2017), "Elsevier Acquires bepress, a Leading Provider of Academic Institutional Repository Solutions", Elsevier Press Releases, URL: https://www.elsevier.com/about/press-releases/corporate/elsevier-acquires-bepress]. This was an "Infrastructure Capture." By owning the software universities use to host their own archives, Elsevier became the landlord for the very "Madisonian Walls" universities had built to protect their intellectual property. The "Forward Compatibility" of academic history is now a hostage to the software roadmap of a commercial vendor. We have built a "Neighborhood of Nowhere," where the "Stones" of discovery have crumbled into 404 errors, leaving a "Zombie Archive" where papers appear authoritative but the support beams have rotted away.
