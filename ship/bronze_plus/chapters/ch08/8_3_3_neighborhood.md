# Semantic Vector Neighborhood: Section 8.3 (Paragraph 3)
## Paragraph Text:
> "This disregard for the record has been formalized through a process I call "Regulatory Over-compliance." Institutions are increasingly utilizing privacy and data-protection laws as a legal shield for the wholesale liquidation of their history. We only know the scale of this "Regulatory Solvent" because of the work of T. Lindsey and the Environmental Data & Governance Initiative (EDGI). In their 2025 audit, *The State of Federal Open Data*, they documented how government agencies began removing decades of environmental impact reports and hydrology tables by citing "data minimization" requirements—a distorted interpretation of the "Right to be Forgotten." As Lindsey observed: "The institutions are not deleting data to protect the privacy of citizens; they are deleting it to reduce their own 'risk surface.' If the data doesn't exist, it cannot be used in a lawsuit, it cannot be used to contradict a current policy, and it doesn't need to be maintained. They are using the language of 'Safety' and 'Compliance' to perform an act of archival arson" [Environmental Data & Governance Initiative (EDGI) (2025), "The State of Federal Open Data: Regulatory Minimization and the Siphoning of Public Science", EDGI Annual Report, URL: https://envirodatagov.org/reports/state-of-federal-open-data-2025/]."
