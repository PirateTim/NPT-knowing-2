Let’s get one thing straight before we begin: **Artificial Intelligence is not intelligent.**

It does not "know" anything. It does not "think." It is a statistical probability engine—a very expensive parrot that predicts the next likely token in a sequence. It works if, and only if, the underlying data is anchored to reality.

I am writing this book because we have cut the anchor.

My cynicism is not born of fear; it is born of architecture. I have spent the last thirty years building the foundations of the digital record. I worked on the architecture that currently hosts over **100 million** pieces of scholarly content—from the American Institute of Physics to the vast vaults of Atypon. I managed the migrations that moved the scientific history of the human race from paper to servers.

I know how fragile the Chain of Custody actually is. And I know that without that chain—without the unsexy, rigorous framework of trust, reliability, and verifiability—data is not knowledge. It is just noise.
