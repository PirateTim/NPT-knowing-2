#### 4.16 The Case of Marimar Martinez
We see the catastrophic result of this training in the case of Marimar Martinez. In 2021, in Laredo, Texas, a Border Patrol agent fired into a moving vehicle, shooting Martinez, an unarmed passenger, in the head. The reality was a "bad shoot", a reckless use of lethal force against a civilian. But the "News" did not report the reality. It reported the Legal Fiction. The initial headlines, transcribed directly from the arrest affidavit, read: "Woman Charged with Assault on Federal Officer" [Laredo Morning Times (2021), "Woman Charged with Assault on Federal Officer at Bridge of the Americas", Laredo Morning Times, URL: https://www.lmtonline.com/local/crime/article/Woman-charged-with-assault-on-federal-officer-16281234.php].

The reporter faced a choice. Choice A (Reporting): Drive to the scene, interview witnesses, examine the bullet holes, and discover the truth (Cost: High. Risk: High). Choice B (Stenography): Download the PDF from the court database, see the charge "Assault," and write the headline (Cost: Zero. Risk: Zero). The reporter chose B. They did not do so out of malice, but out of Efficiency. The Police Department provides a "Content Subsidy"—a steady stream of free, formatted narratives. To reject this subsidy requires work. To accept it requires only a keyboard. The result was that the "Paper of Record" smeared a victim of state violence as a criminal, simply because the shooter filed the paperwork first.
