#### 4.5 The Hockey Stick of Irrelevance
We do not have to guess at the scale of this collapse. We have the receipts. The forensic evidence of this shift is found in the longitudinal data collected by Thomas Patterson at the Harvard Kennedy School. In his seminal analysis Out of Order, Patterson tracked the "Schema" of front-page political reporting over decades. He divided the coverage into two buckets: the Policy Schema (Reporting on what the law does) and the Game Schema (Reporting on who is winning).

The resulting graph is a Hockey Stick of Nihilism. In 1960, during the Kennedy-Nixon era, over 50% of coverage focused on Policy. The press acted as a Referee, enforcing the rules of reality. By 1992, Policy coverage had collapsed to 20%, while Game coverage spiked to 80% [Thomas E. Patterson (1993), "Out of Order", Alfred A. Knopf, URL: https://archive.org/details/outoforder00patt]. By 2024, the "Policy Schema" had effectively vanished, appearing only as a prop in the theater of the "Game" [Thomas E. Patterson (2016), "Pre-Primary News Coverage of the 2016 Presidential Race: Trump's Rise, Sanders' Emergence, Clinton's Struggle", Harvard Kennedy School Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy, URL: https://shorensteincenter.org/pre-primary-news-coverage-2016-presidential-race/]. This was not a result of budget cuts. It was a choice. As Joan Didion observed in Insider Baseball, "What 'the narrative' offers its players is a kind of suspended professional reality... in which the actual problems of the country are converted into 'issues' to be 'handled' by professional managers" [Joan Didion (1988), "Insider Baseball", The New York Review of Books 35(16), URL: https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1988/10/27/insider-baseball/].

The managers of the newsroom ceased to be Editors; they became Talent Agents for the Narcissists. They did not understand the difference between "News" (Contextualized Reality) and "Feed" (Raw Noise). We see this explicitly in the confession of Jeff Zucker, the President of CNN, regarding his decision to air the 2016 Trump rallies live and unfiltered. "I think it was a very useful and important service... by airing the rallies unfiltered" [Jeff Zucker (2017), "Interview with the Harvard Institute of Politics", Harvard Kennedy School Institute of Politics, URL: https://iop.harvard.edu/forum/conversation-jeff-zucker]. This is the language of a man who has lost the ability to distinguish between a fire hose and a drinking fountain. He believed his job was to manage the plumbing, not to filter the water. Or, as CBS CEO Les Moonves famously laughed during the same cycle: "It may not be good for America, but it's damn good for CBS... The money's rolling in and this is fun" [Leslie Moonves (2016), "Remarks at the Morgan Stanley Technology, Media & Telecom Conference", The Hollywood Reporter, URL: https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/les-moonves-donald-trumps-run-871465/].
