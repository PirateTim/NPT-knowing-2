#### 4.7 The AI’s Lesson
When the Large Language Model ingests the archives of 1990–2025, it does not see a decline in quality; it sees a shift in Weights. It sees that for thirty years, the concepts of "Strategy," "Optics," "Polling," and "Messaging" appear with 400% greater frequency than the concepts of "Poverty," "Infrastructure," or "Climate Physics" [Thomas E. Patterson (2016), "Pre-Primary News Coverage of the 2016 Presidential Race: Trump's Rise, Sanders' Emergence, Clinton's Struggle", Harvard Kennedy School Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy, URL: https://shorensteincenter.org/pre-primary-news-coverage-2016-presidential-race/]. It learns that the reaction to an event is statistically more probable than the event itself. The AI is a sociopath because we fed it the diaries of a narcissist. We taught it that Reality is just a "Game," and that the winner is the one who generates the most noise. The "Business Model" collapse that followed—the "dark purple lesion" on the face of the industry—was not the cause of this death. It was the result. The audience didn't leave because of the Internet; they left because the Referee took off his striped shirt, put on a team jersey, and asked the players for a selfie.
