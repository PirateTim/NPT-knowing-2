#### 4.23 The Normalization of Deviance
This ritual has consequences. By accepting the "Pogue Trade" and employing "Secondary Elaborations," the press has fostered a culture of the "Normalization of Deviance." As sociologist Diane Vaughan defined it in her autopsy of the Challenger disaster, this is the process by which a group incrementally accepts higher thresholds of risk and failure until the deviant behavior becomes the organizational norm [Diane Vaughan (1996), "The Challenger Launch Decision: Risky Technology, Culture, and Deviance at NASA", University of Chicago Press, DOI: 10.7208/chicago/9780226346960.001.0001, URL: https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226346960.001.0001].

The "Magic 7" of the AI era rely on this normalization. They rely on a press corps that has forgotten how to be an auditor. When Kara Swisher, the dean of tech journalism, dismisses the dangers of the new oligarchs by stating, "@sama is no different than most of the talented ones" [Kara Swisher (2023), "Soft disagree with this pearl clutching tone...", X / Twitter Post, URL: https://twitter.com/karaswisher/status/1726615498308493489], she is not just offering an opinion; she is signaling the completion of the cycle. The press has moved from the "Five Nines" of the utility age to the "Zero Nines" of the AI age without blinking, because in the Society of the Spectacle, the image of the genius is the only truth that matters. The money may be fake, the product may be broken, but as Joey Ramone reminded us, the show is really out of sight.
