#### 4.9 The Man with No First Language
To understand why the Artificial Intelligence of 2025 cannot distinguish between a policy and a hallucination, we must revisit the moment the American media decided that "incoherence" was a problem to be fixed rather than a fact to be reported. The "Translation Engine" was built in the summer of 2001 to service George W. Bush. Bush was "a man with no first language." He did not speak in linear sentences; he spoke in tangled knots of syntax that often lacked semantic meaning.

When he traveled to Gothenburg, Sweden, in June 2001, he declared: "Africa is a nation that suffers from incredible disease" (CNN, 2001). When he attempted to discuss energy conservation in Denver, he warned the public about "vampire" devices by stating: "A vampire is a... a cell deal you can plug in the wall" [The White House (2001), "Remarks by the President on Energy Conservation", Office of the Press Secretary, The American Presidency Project, URL: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-energy-conservation]. Faced with a Commander-in-Chief who sounded less like a statesman and more like a malfunctioning algorithm, the editors of The New York Times and the Associated Press made a critical decision. They chose Mercy over Reporting. They did not run the headline "President Confuses Continent with Country." Instead, they engaged the "Translation Engine." They polished his garbled syntax into cogent paragraphs. They imposed logic where there was none. They reported: "The President and European leaders discussed the challenge of fighting HIV/AIDS in African nations" [Associated Press (2001), "Bush, European leaders discuss AIDS and Climate at Gothenburg Summit", Associated Press Archive, URL: https://apnews.com/article/e786b3bcf524458da75c0887ee2e92c2].

This was not editing; it was Data Laundering. By fixing the President’s reality model, the press trained the public to believe that the man in charge was operating on a plane of coherence that did not exist. They established the "Bush Protocol": The President’s words are not to be taken literally; they are to be deciphered for intent.
