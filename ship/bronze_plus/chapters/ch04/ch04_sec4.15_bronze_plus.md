#### 4.15 The Grammar of Impunity
The primary tool of this collaboration is the Passive Voice. It is the "Grammar of Impunity," a linguistic device designed to erase the agent of state violence from the historical record. When a citizen shoots a neighbor, the headline is active: "Man Shoots Neighbor." When a police officer shoots a citizen, the headline retreats into a haze of exonerative vagueness: "Officer-Involved Shooting Occurs," or "Suspect Dies After Medical Incident During Police Interaction."

As journalist Radley Balko notes, this is not just bad writing; it is a moral erasure. "The grammatical structure of the police report is designed to remove the agent from the action. It is the language of no-fault murder" [Balko, R. (2018), "The curious grammar of police shootings", The Washington Post, URL: https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/opinions/wp/2018/07/10/the-curious-grammar-of-police-shootings/]. This is Learned Helplessness. The reporter does not write "Officer Smith shot the suspect" because the AP Stylebook and the legal department have trained them to view the police officer not as an actor, but as a force of nature—a weather event that merely "encounters" suspects who then spontaneously die [Johnson, A. (2019), "Episode 80: The 'Officer-Involved' Shooting and the Subversion of Real Police Accountability", Citations Needed Podcast, URL: https://citationsneeded.libsyn.com/episode-80-the-officer-involved-shooting]. As Janine Jackson of FAIR observes, "When you say 'clashes erupted' instead of 'police beat protesters,' you are not being neutral. You are lying for the state" [Jackson, J. (2018), "The Corporate Media’s “Officer-Involved” Active Voice", FAIR CounterSpin, URL: https://fair.org/home/the-corporate-medias-officer-involved-active-voice/].
