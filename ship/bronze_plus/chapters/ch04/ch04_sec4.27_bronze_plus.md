#### 4.27 The Main Character Syndrome
As the institution faded, the "Celebrity Journalist" rose to replace it. The reporter ceased to be a witness and became a Protagonist. This shift from "Invisible Observer" to "Main Character" reached its zenith in the career of CNN’s Jim Acosta. During the first Trump administration, Acosta did not just ask questions; he performed "Resistance." When the White House suspended his press pass in 2018 after a microphone dispute, the story wasn't the administration's evasion of transparency; the story was Jim Acosta.

On November 7, 2018, Acosta tweeted his own internal monologue about the President: "In person he sounds depressed and despondent," transforming the White House beat into a diary of personal grievances [Acosta, J. (2018), "In person he sounds depressed and despondent", Twitter / X Post, URL: https://twitter.com/Acosta/status/1060241088657666048]. This was not reporting; it was main character energy. Even journalism students, the future of the trade, looked at this display and saw not courage, but "grandstanding." In a scathing op-ed for Washington and Lee University's Ring-tum Phi, student journalist Tyler Palicia dismissed Acosta’s performance as "futile" and "hypocritical," noting that the celebrity reporter had become indistinguishable from the partisan hacks he claimed to critique [Palicia, T. (2020), "Opinion: Jim Acosta is a hypocrite", The Ring-tum Phi, URL: https://www.ringtumphi.net/opinion/opinion-jim-acosta-is-a-hypocrite/]. When the students are lecturing the White House Correspondent on the difference between "News" and "Theater," the profession has officially collapsed.
