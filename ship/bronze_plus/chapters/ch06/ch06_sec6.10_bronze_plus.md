### 6.10 The Five Families: Infrastructure of Persistence
Between 1990 and 2010, the global community constructed DOIs and CLOCKSS to ensure knowledge remained verifiable. A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) identifies the object itself [Norman Paskin (2010), "Digital Object Identifier (DOI®) System", Encyclopedia of Library and Information Sciences, Third Edition, Taylor & Francis, pp. 1586–1592, DOI: 10.1081/E-ELIS3-120044418, URL: https://doi.org/10.1081/E-ELIS3-120044418], ensuring the Stones remained fixed in the Wall even as corporate ground shifted.

The AI industry represents a bypass of this infrastructure. Datasets like **C4** and **The Pile** [Leo Gao, Stella Biderman, Sid Black, Laurence Golding, Travis Hoppe, Charles Foster, Jason Phang, Horace He, Anish Thite, Noa Nabeshima, Shawn Presser, and Connor Leahy (2020), "The Pile: An 800GB Dataset of Diverse Text for Language Modeling", EleutherAI / arXiv:2101.00027, DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2101.00027, URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027] were built to ignore the DOI. By violating the metadata governing Open Access, architects performed a mass "Identity Stripping" of the scientific record. This results in **Generation Loss**: a fact becomes a "Plausible Pattern" with no link to its witness.
