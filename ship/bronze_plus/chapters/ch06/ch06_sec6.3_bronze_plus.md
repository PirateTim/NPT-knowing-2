### 6.3 The First Patent Pools
The Madisonian Wall serves as a structural necessity for industrial survival. Historically, the American knowledge system achieved this through the "Patent Pool"—a legal architecture designed to ensure that individual "exclusive Rights" did not degrade into "Rubble." These pools acted as the first APIs of the physical world.

The Sewing Machine Combination of 1856 provides the forensic example. Before this agreement, the industry existed in a state of paralysis known as the "Sewing Machine War." Inventors like Elias Howe and Isaac Singer held the "Stones" of essential components (the lockstitch, the vertical needle). Individually, these patents were evidentiary fragments; combined, they were a machine. By pooling their patents, they rebuilt the Madisonian Wall, acknowledging that the "value" of their discovery was contingent upon the structural integrity of the entire system.

This logic became a matter of national security in 1917. The American aviation industry was crippled by the "Wright-Curtiss" patent feud until the U.S. government brokered the Manufacturers Aircraft Association (MAA). All manufacturers were required to pay a flat fee for access to the entire "Wall" of aviation knowledge. It was a realization that when the "Chain of Custody" for information becomes a barrier to the "Progress of Science," the solution mandates a "Public Protocol" that preserves the pedigree while enabling the use.
