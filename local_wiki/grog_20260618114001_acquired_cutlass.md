# CORE THESIS
The confluence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption and economic uncertainty is causing a significant reduction in entry-level and graduate job openings. This trend, termed a 'jobpocalypse', threatens to create a long-term talent vacuum within companies by disrupting the traditional pipeline for future mid-level and senior roles.

# CLAIMS
- Entry-level jobs are disappearing at an "alarming rate."
- The current period is described as "one of the most challenging times in history for graduates to get a job."
- The causes are identified as a "perfect storm" of AI, political instability, and economic uncertainty.
- For the first time on record, the rate of joblessness among graduates has surpassed the overall unemployment rate.
- Major graduate employers are reducing the number of jobs they offer.
- A potential future corporate structure is a "diamond shape" with few entry-level roles, which raises questions about the source of future mid-level employees.
- Companies have paused graduate hiring for approximately 2 to 2.5 years due to economic forecasting challenges.
- This hiring pause is predicted to create a "void of talent" in the next 2-3 years, potentially leading to the over-promotion of existing staff.
- In the US market, an "entry-level" applicant is often expected to have 1-2 years of experience.
- Companies are delaying entry-level hiring to assess the impact of AI.
- The decline in entry-level job postings is three times faster than for senior positions.
- Industries like accounting and law are heavily impacted, with suggestions that AI could perform 80% of tasks done by junior lawyers.
- Law firms with a high average employee age (e.g., 55) risk their existence within 15 years if they do not hire junior talent.
- Companies that have replaced staff with AI have seen "varied" results, suggesting the transition is not straightforward.
- AI is expected to automate a large amount of commoditized, data-oriented, and administrative work in the legal field within 3-5 years.
- Cutting the graduate pipeline is described as "really dangerous" for the future of a company.
- AI can be used to move junior employees "up the value chain" by automating repetitive "drudge work."
- Effective use of AI requires new skills, specifically prompt engineering.
- Employees are advised to be "a little bit scared" and remain flexible, as some jobs will disappear.
- The current education system is not adequately equipping graduates with the skills needed for future workplaces.
- Historical models, such as paid apprenticeships or indentured servitude, are being considered as potential solutions for training junior talent whose skills are not immediately monetizable.
