#### 5.20 The Looters (The Economics of Noise)
The Slop wasn't an accident; it was the terminal stage of Cognitive Capitalism. As the political theorist Jodi Dean argued two decades prior, the internet shifted the unit of value from the "Message" (Content) to the "Contribution" (Metabolism). The system ceased to care what you said, only that you said it, generating the metadata required to sell ads [Dean, Jodi (2005), "Communicative Capitalism: Circulation and the Foreclosure of Politics", Cultural Politics, Vol. 1, No. 1, pp. 51-74, DOI: 10.2752/174321905778054845, URL: https://doi.org/10.2752/174321905778054845]. For years, we believed the "Fantasy of Abundance"—that more voices meant more democracy. In reality, the abundance was the weapon. The volume of noise became so high that it functioned as a Denial of Service attack on human cognition.

This economic logic led inevitably to what Cory Doctorow termed "Enshittification". Platforms like Google, Amazon, and Reddit, having captured the market, began to systematically degrade their own products to extract the last ounce of value [Doctorow. (2023). The Enshittification of TikTok. Wired., URL: https://www.wired.com/story/tiktok-enshittification/]. Google Search, once the library of the world, became a wasteland of SEO-spam and AI-generated summaries that hallucinated answers. They stopped "organizing the world's information" and started "monetizing the world's confusion". The consequence was the Memory Hole. As platforms decayed and link-rot accelerated to save server costs, the "Archive" of the early web vanished. We entered a "Perpetual Present" where history is rewritten daily by the latest SEO update. You can no longer "Look it up." You can only look up what the algorithm wants to sell you today.
