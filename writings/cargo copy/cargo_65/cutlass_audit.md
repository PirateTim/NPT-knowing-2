### Stage 1: Pre-existing Decay
Long before generative AI, the corporate and managerial class cultivated a deep structural contempt for embodied human expertise, institutional apprenticeship, and labor. Professional knowledge was increasingly treated not as an embodied, relational craft developed through years of practice, but as an extractable, commodifiable set of written instructions. The asset highlights the long-standing managerial impulse to eliminate human workers ("pesky employees") by substituting mechanized abstractions for human judgment, reflecting a chronic decay in how institutions value the relational and sensory foundations of knowing.

### Stage 2: Technological Catalyst
Large Language Models arrive as the technical accelerant. By processing vast, ungrounded scraped text corpuses into statistical next-token distributions, LLMs simulate the cadence and syntactic authority of human expertise without possessing any physical, sensory, or contextual grounding. This enables technocrats and venture capitalists to conflate syntactic fluency with epistemic competence, scaling the illusion that "knowledge" can be fully decoupled from living practitioners and automated at scale.

### Stage 3: Proactive Negligence

As the author notes, presenting raw, unverified statistical output as authoritative expertise is "a positive danger to society."
