## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Audit of Causal Claims & Evidentiary Validity
1. **The "Nascent Sandbox" Claim:** Google asserts that because the generative AI market is "emerging" and Google has "to date gained [no] monopoly or market power" in AI specifically, remedies addressing search distribution cannot logically or legally apply to Gemini.
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* **Ad Hoc Immunizing Stratagem**. Counsel establishes an arbitrary semantic boundary between "search" and "AI" to immunize distribution chokeholds from falsification. In reality, Gemini is deployed as a replacement search/retrieval engine. Siloing AI into an abstract "separate market" ignores that the delivery vector is identical to the illegal search distribution channels already adjudicated.
2. **The "Tu Quoque Competitive Parity" Claim:** Google claims that restricting Gemini bundling severely handicaps the company relative to Microsoft, which bundles Copilot with Office products.
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* **Inductive Illusion & Tu Quoque Fallacy**. The claim assumes that the existence of parallel monopolistic bundling by an enterprise competitor renders tying practices benign or necessary for competitive equilibrium, rather than recognizing that unconstrained bundling cascades across the industry, compounding epistemic harm.
3. **The "Non-Monopoly App" Shield:** Counsel asserts that Maps and YouTube are not "monopoly products," and therefore compelling OEMs to install Gemini to access Maps and YouTube does not constitute abusive tying.
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* **Empirically Falsified / Causal Inversion**. Judge Mehta's own evidentiary finding highlights that leveraging essential, dominant consumer utilities to coerce OEM compliance operates via the exact same leverage mechanisms Google deployed to establish its illegal search monopoly.

### Itemized Fallacies Detected
- **Special Pleading ("AI is Different"):** Asserting that novel technological branding absolves an enterprise from foundational principles of anti-tying law, market power leverage, and verification duty.
- **Definitional Retreat / Arbitrary Boundary Framing:** Retreating into hyper-narrow product classifications ("AI market power") while ignoring that the physical vehicle of dominance—the mobile operating system and OEM pre-installation contract—is identical.
- **Tu Quoque (Whataboutism):** Citing Microsoft Copilot’s integration into Windows/Office to justify and normalize coercive distribution agreements for consumer mobile hardware.
