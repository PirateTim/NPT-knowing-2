## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Causal Claim Deconstruction
- **Causal Claim 1 (Tight Coupling & Fragility):** Increasing connectivity and systemic interdependence linearly increase society's exposure to catastrophic single-point failures (e.g., CrowdStrike, WannaCry).  
  *Evidentiary Warrant:* Empirically corroborated by material incidents where a single unvetted software update incapacitated global aviation, banking, and emergency healthcare. The warrant holds under Charles Perrow's normal accident framework.
- **Causal Claim 2 (Catalytic Acceleration):** Generative AI acts as an accelerant ("into overdrive") on top of latent infrastructural vulnerability rather than an isolated, exogenous threat.  
  *Evidentiary Warrant:* Corroborated by the operational realities of synthetic text pollution, automated vulnerability exploitation, and epistemic drift.
- **Causal Claim 3 (The Crisis Precondition):** No meaningful policy or structural reform will occur without a prior catastrophic shock.  
  *Popperian Demarcation Status:* Borderline unfalsifiable immunizing stratagem. If a collapse occurs, the hypothesis is confirmed; if a collapse does not occur, the author asserts society is merely "scheduled to wait it out on the edge of a knife." However, as a sociopolitical observation of institutional inertia, it mirrors historical regulatory patterns.

### Detected Fallacies & Structural Flaws
- **Historical Revisionism / False Distinction:** The text asserts that *"in the lead-up to global financial crisis, the economy wasn’t lighting up with warning signs. With the current AI economic bubble, the risk is clear as day."* This is an ungrounded historical assertion. The 2008 crash was preceded by voluminous, explicit empirical warnings (e.g., Brooksley Born, Nouriel Roubini, Raghuram Rajan) that were actively ignored by captured regulators. Conflating willful blindness with an "absence of warning signs" obscures the deliberate nature of proactive negligence.
- **Secondary Provenance Severance:** As a typical *Futurism* blog summary, the piece attributes Curran's claims to an op-ed in *The Conversation* rather than citing Curran’s underlying academic research or empirical sociotechnical datasets, forcing the reader to rely on academic title authority (*argumentum ad verecundiam* mediated through secondary aggregation).
