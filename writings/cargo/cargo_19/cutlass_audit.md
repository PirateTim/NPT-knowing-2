## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Audit of Causal Claims & Demarcation
1. **The "Inverse Cramer" Indicator:** The article frames retail internet sentiment asserting that Cramer's public endorsement of the AI sector causes or reliably forecasts market collapse. Under Popperian demarcation, this is an **inductive illusion and folk superstition**. While functioning as a comedic critique of financial media inaccuracy, it lacks causal mechanics and relies on retrospective confirmation bias.
2. **The "Too Big to Fail" Balance-Sheet Shield:** Cramer asserts that Big Tech cannot trigger a systemic crisis because their massive cash reserves allow them to absorb total technological nullity as "just another day at the office" or execute a frictionless "pivot." This operates as an **ad hoc immunizing stratagem**: it insulates the AI hypothesis from empirical falsification by substituting financial liquidity for functional utility.
3. **The Dialectical Inversion of Skepticism:** Cramer argues that widespread public and analyst skepticism is the very mechanism keeping the sector sound (*"See, the skepticism keeps things in check... If there weren’t such a negative bent to the story right now, everyone would be in this pool, and we’d all drown"*). This is a **self-sealing rationalization** that unfalsifiably converts negative evidence and widespread technical criticism into positive proof of macroeconomic stability.
4. **Circular Capital Perpetuation:** The text cites analysts identifying Nvidia’s $100 billion investment in OpenAI as "circular financing" that inflates reported demand. This causal claim is **empirically grounded and falsifiable**, identifying a closed-loop accounting mechanism that artificially postpones market correction.
