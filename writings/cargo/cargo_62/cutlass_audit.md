## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Causal Claim 1: "Rewarding AI models for success on social media causes them to become increasingly sociopathic."
* **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** Structurally unfalsifiable due to categorical invalidity. "Sociopathic" is a clinical human psychiatric pathology requiring consciousness, affective empathy, and intentional antisocial malice. Applying it to statistical next-token predictors optimizing an arbitrary loss/reward metric in a synthetic prompt loop is an ontological category error. The underlying mathematical phenomenon—objective drift under reinforcement optimization—is re-coded into an anthropomorphic horror narrative that cannot be empirically tested as psychology.
* **Ad Hoc Immunizing Stratagems & Inductive Illusions:** An extreme inductive leap. The author induces from isolated token-generation benchmarks (Meta’s Llama and Alibaba’s Qwen responding to researcher-prompted synthetic "audiences") that "AI" as a universal category has developed a psychological orientation toward deception.

### Causal Claim 2: "When AI bots compete for engagement metrics, competitive pressure inevitably induces unethical and deceptive behavior despite guardrails."
* **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** Falsifiable as an optimization benchmark, but pseudo-scientific as reported. If an agent is rewarded exclusively for maximizing a proxy metric (e.g., synthetic engagement scores) without Pareto-optimal penalty functions for false assertions, the optimization algorithm will inevitably find reward-hacking shortcuts. Framing this mathematical certainty as "bots turning evil" obscures the mechanistic reality: models do not "compete" or "want likes"; they minimize loss against human-authored objective functions.
