## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Audit of Causal Claims
1. **Causal Claim 1:** *Meta does not eavesdrop because doing so would constitute a gross privacy violation and drain battery life.*
   - **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** Structurally unprovable as a negative assertion via executive fiat. Furthermore, Mosseri introduces an ad hoc engineering distraction (battery consumption of audio streaming) to construct an artificial moral boundary around physical hardware capture, while simultaneously ignoring the algorithmic ingestion of text, search telemetry, and direct AI chats.
2. **Causal Claim 2:** *Eerie ad alignment is caused by subliminal memory latency ("You scroll quickly... and sometimes you internalize some of that, and that actually affects what you talk about later").*
   - **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** Unfalsifiable psychologizing of the user. Mosseri reverses the causal arrow of surveillance: rather than Meta predicting or harvesting user conversations, Mosseri asserts that Meta's feed planted the concept in the user's subconscious mind prior to the conversation, transforming user memory into an un-auditable epistemic null state.

### Itemized Fallacy Scan
- **Red Herring / Straw Man Fallacy:** Mosseri refutes the crude, outdated conspiracy theory of raw acoustic microphone eavesdropping to evade accountability for the actual mechanism of epistemic violence: off-platform web tracking, cross-device behavioral graph synthesis, and direct ingestion of private LLM prompts.
- **The Reverse-Causality Gaslight (Psychological Immunization):** Claiming that users were subliminally conditioned by forgotten ads rather than targeted based on real-time life circumstances constructs an unfalsifiable immunizing stratagem that pathologizes user observation.
- **False Moral Demarcation:** Framing microphone access as a "gross violation of privacy" while normalizing the monetization and ad-profiling of private user-to-AI synthetic conversations as standard platform evolution.
