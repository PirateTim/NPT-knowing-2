## 1. Epistemic Decoupling & Chain of Ruin

### Epistemic Decoupling: SAYS vs. IS
- **What the Asset SAYS:** The generative AI infrastructure boom is hurtling toward an unavoidable financial catastrophe. Based on updated "napkin math" from hedge fund manager Harris Kupperman (Praetorian Capital), data center components (specifically AI accelerator chips) depreciate and degrade physically or become technologically obsolete within 3 to 10 years rather than the standard 10-year curve. Consequently, break-even requirements for 2025 alone demand $320B–$480B in revenue against an actual industry run-rate of ~$20B. When projected into 2026, cumulative capital expenditures will require over $1 trillion in revenue just to break even, scaling an industry-level misallocation into a national macroeconomic crisis.
- **What the Asset IS:** A tertiary aggregative journalistic digest (Futurism summarizing Morningstar summarizing a personal investment Substack blog post) that launders unverified hedge fund back-of-the-envelope projections and anonymous telephone hearsay into authoritative tech-doom reporting. While diagnosing a genuine macroeconomic and physical reality (the astronomical disparity between capital expenditure and realized software utility), the article itself operates as an epistemic parasite—providing zero primary financial audit links, zero hardware telemetry data, zero direct citations to SEC 10-K filings, and zero independent reporting beyond secondary aggregation.

## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Causal Assertions & Deductive Integrity
1. **Causal Claim 1:** *Accelerated silicon obsolescence and thermal degradation shorten AI data center depreciation curves to 3–10 years, causing the return-on-investment horizon to mathematically collapse.*  
   - **Audit:** Causally plausible and physically grounded in semiconductor physics and thermal throttling. However, the article establishes this claim not through empirical lab degradation tests or audited hyperscaler depreciation schedules, but via private conversations between a hedge fund manager and unnamed industry contacts.
2. **Causal Claim 2:** *The disparity between projected capital expenditure ($1T cumulative across 2025–2026) and annual software revenue (~$20B) means that scaling infrastructure converts an industry crisis into a national economic crisis.*  
   - **Audit:** Strong macro-structural validity. If capital markets absorb $1T in non-performing debt and equity allocations predicated on phantom productivity gains, debt-service failure threatens broader credit markets and municipal utility balance sheets.
3. **Causal Claim 3:** *Widespread confusion among data center operators regarding their own business model proves an imminent market collapse.*  
   - **Audit:** Non-sequitur / Inductive leap. Widespread operator confusion indicates irrational exuberance and speculative malinvestment, but market timing and collapse vectors depend on credit conditions, sovereign subsidies, and enterprise contract lock-ins, not merely the psychological bafflement of engineers.

### Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability
- **Falsifiability:** The core economic claim is strictly falsifiable: it can be refuted if enterprise AI revenue reaches $320B–$480B in 2025/2026, or if hyperscalers successfully extend the productive operational lifespan of H100 clusters beyond 7–10 years through secondary inference workloads.
- **Ad Hoc Immunizing Stratagems:** The article relies on shifting investor goalposts. When Kupperman’s original 10-year curve proved untenable, he reduced it to 3–10 years based on informal conversations, allowing the timeline of the catastrophe to become an elastic, unfalsifiable horizon of perpetual imminent collapse.

### Itemized Epistemic & Logical Fallacies
- **Provenance Severance (*Epistemic Taxonomy Mode 4*):** The article cites Morningstar and Kupperman’s blog post but fails to provide direct bibliographic citations, links to the underlying financial models, or pointers to the primary Morningstar dispatch. The reader is forced to trust the author's synthesis of another author's synthesis of a blogger's phone calls.
- **The Naive Napkin-Math Fallacy:** Elevating unvetted, speculative arithmetic from an incentivized hedge fund investor into an undisputed macroeconomic benchmark without stress-testing assumptions regarding energy arbitrage or tax amortization write-offs.
- **Argumentum ad Populum (Echo-Chamber Consensus):** *"Overall, the pessimists broadly agree: it's no longer a matter of if AI is massively overhyped, but when the whole thing comes crashing down."* This substitutes the consensus of the "pessimist" cohort for empirical proof of collapse dynamics.
- **Anecdotal Induction (Hasty Generalization):** Deriving the global macroeconomic fate of cloud computing from informal chats with "over two-dozen rather senior people in the datacenter universe."
