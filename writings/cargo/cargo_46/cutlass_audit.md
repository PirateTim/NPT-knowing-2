## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Causal Claims & Evidentiary Warrants
1. **Causal Claim:** Traditional enterprise AI adoption fails because leaders treat AI as process tools rather than designing comprehensive "intelligence environments" grounded in philosophy.
   - **Evidentiary Warrant:** Anecdotal executive sentiment ("overwhelmed by AI capabilities but underwhelmed by AI returns") coupled with abstract theoretical assertions from the authors' ongoing "Philosophy Eats AI" essay series.
   - **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** **Unfalsifiable.** This constitutes an ad hoc immunizing stratagem. If an AI deployment fails to generate value, the authors can claim post-hoc that the organization simply lacked "foundational philosophical clarity" or failed to build a "genuine intelligence environment." There is no measurable empirical threshold at which an "environment" is deemed properly designed versus flawed.
2. **Causal Claim:** Stephen Wolfram’s principle of computational irreducibility explains why enterprise AI cannot be predicted beforehand and proves that organizations must run complex systems empirically to discover how value is generated.
   - **Evidentiary Warrant:** A single quote from Stephen Wolfram explaining that one must run a complex computational system to observe its behavior.
   - **Popperian Demarcation & Falsifiability:** **Epistemic Category Error / Conceptual Smuggling.** Wolfram’s principle of computational irreducibility is a formal mathematical theorem derived from deterministic cellular automata (e.g., Rule 30) demonstrating that no computational shortcut exists to predict the $n$-th state. Repurposing this mathematical concept to explain enterprise workflow chaos or to excuse the unreliability of stochastic LLMs conflates *deterministic non-computability* with *probabilistic unreliability*.

### Itemized Fallacy Scan (Formal & Epistemic)
- **Ad Hoc Immunizing Stratagem (Defensio Post-Hoc):** Protecting generative AI systems from empirical falsification by moving the goalposts from tool functionality to organizational "environment design."
- **Equivocation & Category Smuggling:** Conflating Wolfram's deterministic, symbolic computation (Mathematica, Wolfram Alpha) with probabilistic language models. Wolfram Alpha succeeds precisely because it is symbolic, verified, and rule-bound; enterprise generative AI fails precisely because it is statistical, ungrounded, and stochastic.
- **No True Scotsman Fallacy:** Setting up an unfalsifiable distinction between mere "knowledge graphs" or "process tools" and a "genuine intelligence environment." Any failure of AI in the wild is dismissed as not being a "genuine" integration.
