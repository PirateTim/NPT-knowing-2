## 2. Causal Claims & Popperian Demarcation Audit

### Causal Claim Deconstruction
1. **Claim 1 (Empirical / Falsifiable):** The geographical concentration of AI data centers directly drives localized electricity price spikes of up to 267%.
   - *Evidentiary Warrant:* Bloomberg empirical analysis across tens of thousands of nodal electricity pricing points in the US over a five-year baseline.
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* Strictly falsifiable through Locational Marginal Pricing (LMP) data, utility rate filings, and public utility commission dockets. Passed.
2. **Claim 2 (Projective / Macroeconomic):** Without regulatory or structural mitigation, data center power demands will systematically inflate living costs for all residential rate-payers across the United States.
   - *Evidentiary Warrant:* Attributed to an anonymous "major investor."
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* Testable and falsifiable over time by tracking capital expenditure rider pass-throughs on consumer utility bills in jurisdictions with dense hyperscaler interconnection queues. Passed as a testable economic hypothesis, though weakened by anonymous sourcing.
3. **Claim 3 (Promissory / Speculative):** The continuation of AI scaling laws necessitates 250 gigawatts of continuous electrical generation.
   - *Evidentiary Warrant:* Unverified corporate floating by Sam Altman, reported via secondary reporting in *The Information*.
   - *Popperian Demarcation:* **Fails demarcation.** Functions as an ungrounded, non-falsifiable promissory assertion. It acts as an inductive illusion that treats arbitrary algorithmic compute-scaling projections as physical inevitabilities, completely unmoored from thermodynamic feasibility, grid physics, or demonstrable cognitive utility.

### Itemized Detected Fallacies
- **Appeal to Anonymous Authority (*Argumentum Ad Verecundiam* Variant):** Relying on "one major investor" to deliver the critical structural warning shields the financial actor from disclosing their own market positioning, regulatory lobbying, or grid-arbitrage investments.
- **The Promissory Scale Fallacy (Inductive Megalomania):** Framing a speculative compute demand (250 GW—one-third of the continental US grid) as an inevitable baseline requirement for technology, laundering corporate ambition into an unavoidable macroeconomic imperative.
- **Agnotological Externalization Omission:** Treating electricity cost increases as an ambient act of nature ("data centers sucking up all the load") rather than identifying the proactive contractual arrangements between utilities and hyperscalers that deliberately offload grid reinforcement costs onto captive consumers.
