### Causal Assertions & Epistemic Demarcation
1. **Causal Claim:** Consulting creative professionals actively eliminated generic or repetitive outputs from the generator (*"Microsoft says it sought feedback from creative professionals in order to avoid 'repetitive or generically-stylized outputs.'"*).
   - **Popperian Demarcation:** **Unfalsifiable Corporate Assertion.** The cohort of creative professionals, the feedback methodology, the evaluation prompts, and the dataset pruning criteria are entirely undisclosed. 2. **Causal Claim:** Ranking in the top 10 of LMArena establishes verified technical competence and human preference (*"The model has already secured a spot in the top 10 of LMArena, the AI benchmark site where humans compare outputs from different systems and vote on the best one."*).
   - **Popperian Demarcation:** **Inductive Illusion / Goodhart's Law.** Elo ratings derived from anonymous web votes on unstandardized, subjective visual prompts measure transient aesthetic novelty ("vibe checks") rather than empirical accuracy, semantic coherence, or compositional fidelity. Leaderboard placement is substituted for scientific verification.
3. **Causal Claim:** Developing MAI-Image-1, MAI-Voice-1, and chatbot MAI-1-preview establishes Microsoft's strategic independence and internal model sovereignty.
   - **Popperian Demarcation:** **Epistemic Arbitrage via Ephemeral Demonstration.** Announcing prototype tools does not establish operational independence from foundation model vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic). The announcement functioned as an ephemeral corporate roadmap that dissolved within six months when the underlying product line proved non-viable.

- **The Guild Stenography Fallacy (SHARED-HEURISTIC-014):** Confusing the procedural attribution of unverified executive statements with the execution of journalistic investigation.
