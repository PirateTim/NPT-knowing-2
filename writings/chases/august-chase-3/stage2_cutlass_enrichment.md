### Stage 1: Pre-Existing Decay (Institutional Erosion Prior to AI)
Long before the deployment of Large Language Models, legal education experienced severe epistemic erosion:
* **Digital Stenography over Active Reasoning:** The introduction of laptops into law school classrooms over the past two decades transformed the Socratic method into a race for verbatim lecture transcription. Students ceased engaging in real-time doctrinal synthesis, relying instead on shared commercial outlines (BarBri, Lexis, Emmanuel) and crowd-sourced class notes.
* **Devaluation of Deep Drafting:** The law school curriculum had already reduced rigorous research and writing to isolated 1L units, while commercial legal practice increasingly relied on boilerplate templates, automated discovery tools, and copy-paste pleading mechanics driven by billable hour optimizations.
* **Loss of Empirical Lineage:** Legal scholarship and student legal writing increasingly rewarded citation stacking and rhetorical posturing over empirical verification, creating an environment ripe for automated mimicry.

### Stage 2: Technological Catalyst (LLM Acceleration of Epistemic Collapse)
Generative AI arrived in 2022–2026 not as a novel corruptor, but as an accelerant that exposed pre-existing vulnerabilities:
* **Computational Truthiness (`CUTLASS-RULE-017`):** LLMs generate fluent, authoritative legal prose, complete with plausible case citations and statutory frameworks, based on statistical pattern prediction rather than doctrinal comprehension.
* **Provenance Destruction:** The ease with which LLMs draft legal briefs, memos, and law review articles completely severed the link between a submitted written document and the cognitive labor of the student whose name appears on the title page. Written legal submissions lost all evidentiary value as proof of student competence.

### Stage 3: Proactive Negligence & Performative Remediation
UChicago Law's strategy exemplifies **Paradigm-Constrained Remediation** (`CUTLASS-RULE-015`):
* **Analog Retreat as Quarantine:** By banning laptops in 1L classes and requiring in-person paper exams and oral defenses, UChicago Law admits that digital submission channels are epistemically un-verifiable. To prove a student possesses a human mind, they must strip away technology entirely and revert to medieval oral disputation and blue-book examinations.
* **The "AI Auditor" Fallacy:** The policy promises to teach upper-level students to "audit" and "evaluate" AI output in 2L/3L electives and the Law School's AI Lab. This relies on the false premise that students who have spent only one year learning analog law can effectively spot subtle hallucinated doctrine or statistical errors produced by enterprise LLMs.
* **Institutional Blind Spot:** While UChicago Law claims it is preparing students for "the AI era," the broader legal industry (law firm management and corporate legal departments) is actively using AI to eliminate the very entry-level associate positions where legal research and document auditing were traditionally learned. Banning laptops in school does not solve the economic obliteration of the legal apprenticeship pipeline.
