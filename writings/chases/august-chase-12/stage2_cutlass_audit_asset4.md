## 4. Formal Epistemic Fallacy & Structural Logic Scan

### A. The "Bubble Epistemology" & Sampling Bias (Informal Fallacy)
- **Mechanic:** The author initially doubts the decline of reading because her local indie bookstore is bustling and her sisters recommend books.
- **Audit:** The author openly acknowledges this as an epistemic blind spot ("*until November 2016 I didn't know I lived in a liberal-elite bubble. I suppose I'm in a book bubble too*"). This self-correction elevates the artifact from Bilge to a high-value vernacular witness (Jib).

### B. Confusing Format Pluralism with Cognitive Parity (Equivocation)
- **Mechanic:** Commenters argue that "storytelling is alive and well in video games, streaming shows, and podcasts," implying that consuming audio/visual narrative exercises the same cognitive faculties as decoding written syntax.
- **Audit:** Equivocation on the term "reading." As the original *Atlantic* thesis and Neil Postman (*Amusing Ourselves to Death*) demonstrate, reading complex linear print requires rigorous internal cognitive projection, metaphor deciphering, and syntactical analysis, whereas audiovisual and podcast consumption relies on passive external simulation and oral redundancy.

### C. The Jane Addams Hull House Contrast (Historical Signal)
- **Mechanic:** A commenter notes: *"I shake my head remembering that at Hull House Jane Addams had debating societies and reading circles of very serious topics and very serious books. Immigrants debating, learning English to really dig deep and read. Today? Can you even imagine a debating society anywhere?"*
- **Audit:** High epistemic signal. Highlights historical proof that rigorous literacy was once a working-class tool for political emancipation and dialectical debate, whereas contemporary digital literacy has been commodified into passive social feed consumption.
