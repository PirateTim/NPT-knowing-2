Title: The Blue Check Isn't Enough Anymore
Author: Shae Omonijo

The digital public square is undergoing a quiet, fundamental breakdown in how authority, credibility, and trust are signaled. For over a decade, the blue checkmark—most famously on Twitter/X—served as a centralized, institutional stamp of verified identity. While flawed and often accused of gatekeeping, it established a basic baseline: the person behind the account was who they claimed to be, or represented the institution they claimed to represent.

When platforms monetized verification—converting the blue check from an audited identity stamp into an $8/month subscription product—they severed the link between the badge and authentic provenance. Overnight, verification ceased to signal identity or expertise; it signaled purchasing power, algorithmic priority, and platform alignment. 

The consequences have been immediate and systemic:
1. Inflation and Degradation of Trust Signals: When anyone can buy a verification badge, the badge no longer verifies anything. The blue checkmark transformed from a mark of authenticity into a badge of platform sycophancy or commercial promotion.
2. The Flood of Verified Slop and Impersonation: Bad actors, spammers, and automated bot networks quickly realized that buying blue checks unlocked algorithmic distribution, allowing paid synthetic noise to drown out legitimate, verified human sources.
3. The Search for New Credibility Anchors: Because the blue check isn't enough anymore, users, newsrooms, and institutions are forced to scramble for secondary and tertiary verification mechanisms—byline history, external domain cross-linking, peer network validation, or decentralized identity protocols.

We have entered an era where institutional trust indicators have been commodified and hollowed out. When verification becomes a revenue feature rather than a trust backstop, the entire information ecosystem devolves into an arena of computational truthiness and pay-to-play authority.
