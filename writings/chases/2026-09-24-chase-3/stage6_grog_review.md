## 4. Meaning Distortion & Semantic Slippage Audit

While the substantive analysis is exceptionally rigorous, two specific items of drift are flagged for reconciliation:

### A. Provenance Metadata Byline Attribution Slip (Minor)
- **The Asset Text:** The cargo asset is an investigative exclusive written by **Layla A. Jones** and published by **Talking Points Memo (TPM)** on September 18, 2026 (`https://talkingpointsmemo.com/news/exclusive-census-bureau-directs-staff-to-keep-quiet-during-comment-period-for-controversial-new-rule`).
- **Scallywag's Text:** In the header metadata block and Section VII Methodological Provenance box, Scallywag writes:
  > *"Canonical Cargo Asset: Associated Press Exclusive on Census Bureau Employee Gag Orders"*  
  > *"Empirical Asset Base: Associated Press Investigative Exclusive (Mike Schneider, Suitland, MD)"*
- **Forensic Finding:** Scallywag conflated the publisher/byline of the primary leak scoop with veteran AP Census reporter Mike Schneider. While Schneider frequently covers Census Bureau beats out of Florida and Maryland, this specific leaked internal email scoop belongs to TPM's Layla A. Jones. This is a journalistic attribution error in the metadata block, though it does not contaminate the empirical facts of the directive itself.
- **Deduction:** -0.7 points.

### B. Procedural Scope Expansion of the Gag Directive (Minor)
- **The Asset Text:** The leaked email specifically ordered staff to:
  1. *"halt all external events related to the 2030 Census"* during the 30-day public comment window; and
  2. submit written justification memos to "George, Michael and John" to attend any external gatherings, even those with agendas unrelated to the 2030 Census (e.g., annual research conferences).
- **Scallywag's Text:** In Toulmin Warrant 01 and Section I, Scallywag states that leadership:
  > *"explicitly instructed civil service researchers, statisticians, and demographers to refrain from submitting public comments..."*
- **Forensic Finding:** Under federal administrative practice, career agency employees generally do not submit formal public comments in the Federal Register docket against their own agency's proposed rules in their official capacity; rather, their official input is traditionally delivered via internal memos and technical working groups. The leaked directive halted *external events and appearances* (depriving the public and external stakeholders of direct demographer access) and subjected all conference participation to vetting. While the functional legal effect of this order—combined with the threat of Schedule F reclassification—was indeed to suppress demographer dissent from the rulemaking process, Scallywag's phrasing implies an explicit textual prohibition on docket comment filings that was not literally stated in the leaked email excerpt.
- **Deduction:** -0.5 points.
