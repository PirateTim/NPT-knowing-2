## 3. The 3-Stage Chain of Ruin Forensic Analysis

### Stage 1: Pre-existing Decay (Institutional Capacity Erosion)
Long before the current AI wave, state regulatory agencies and legislative bodies suffered decades of capacity erosion. Legislative staff were downsized, regulatory authority was outsourced to elite management consultancies, and public oversight bodies lost the technical expertise required to audit complex software architectures. Notice-and-comment rulemaking devolved into administrative ritual, leaving public institutions fundamentally unprepared to police automated systems.

### Stage 2: Technological Catalyst (Unanchored Statistical Scale)
The rapid deployment of probabilistic LLMs and automated prediction tools acts as a powerful accelerant. Systems operating without verifiable ground truth or clear evidentiary chains are integrated across hiring, credit, and public administration. The sheer velocity and opacity of these models outpace traditional statutory review, converting pre-existing administrative weakness into an acute crisis of public governance.

### Stage 3: Proactive Negligence (Substitution of Audits for Liability)
Faced with technological acceleration, credentialed leadership—both corporate executives and university-affiliated policy elites—proactively adopt performative self-regulatory frameworks. Rather than enforcing strict liability, verifying training data provenance, or enforcing labor protections, leadership chooses to adopt voluntary indexes (e.g., "Prosocial AI Index") and procurement checklists. This deliberate choice enables corporate entities to burn tokens and deploy unverified software while shielding themselves behind administrative cover.

## 4. Forensic Scan & Epistemic Taxonomy Failure Modes

### 1. Paradigm-Constrained Remediation (`CUTLASS-RULE-015`)
* **Manifestation:** The author, operating from within elite business school academia, diagnoses global regulatory fragmentation and prescribes a self-developed, soft-law management rubric (the "Prosocial AI Index").
* **Epistemic Failure:** The solution originates from the same managerial paradigm that caused the regulatory deficit. It assumes corporate actors can self-audit their way to "prosocial" outcomes without addressing the underlying business models driven by data extraction, labor degradation, and provenance severance.

### 2. View from Nowhere & False Equivalence (`SHARED-RULE-004` / `Epistemic Taxonomy`)
* **Manifestation:** The asset presents constitutional rights, state purchasing leverage, and voluntary C-suite indexes as co-equal "philosophies" in a neutral menu of regulatory options.
* **Epistemic Failure:** It flattens the fundamental contradiction between state sovereignty/human rights and unregulated capital accumulation, treating regulatory capture and corporate evasion as mere stylistic choices in global governance.

### 3. Regulatory Capture via Performative Frameworks (`CUTLASS-RULE-012`)
* **Manifestation:** The article emphasizes vendor procurement checklists and internal corporate rubrics over enforceable statutory prohibitions or civil/criminal liability for algorithmic harms.
* **Epistemic Failure:** By promoting compliance-as-a-service rubrics, the artifact serves the interests of technology firms and consulting practices, allowing companies to maintain the appearance of compliance while continuing ungrounded automated operations.

### 4. Agnotological Narrative Misdirection (`CUTLASS-RULE-018`)
* **Manifestation:** Reframing systemic harms (algorithmic bias, data theft, labor replacement, epistemic erosion) as "exposure profiles" and "strategic risk factors" for C-suite management.
* **Epistemic Failure:** Converts public harms into private corporate risk management, obscuring the physical reality of resource consumption, labor exploitation, and knowledge destruction.
