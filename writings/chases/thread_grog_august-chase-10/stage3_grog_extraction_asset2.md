### 3. Summary
The article examines the strategic motivations behind the recurring publication of philosophical manifestos by chief executives of major artificial intelligence corporations, including Marc Andreessen, Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, and Mark Zuckerberg. 

The author identifies three primary structural functions of these documents: (1) shaping public and labor sentiment against macroeconomic warnings (such as IMF displacement forecasts), (2) preempting government regulatory frameworks by framing corporate approaches on executive terms, and (3) securing intellectual authority and recruitment leverage by styling corporate leaders as "businessman-philosophers." The piece specifically focuses on Mark Zuckerberg's open-source framing for Meta's Llama models, contrasting this approach with closed lab ecosystems and analyzing it against international competition from Chinese open-weight models (Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM) and European developers (Mistral). The author concludes that these texts operate as competitive instruments targeting regulators, investors, and industry specialists rather than mass public communication.
