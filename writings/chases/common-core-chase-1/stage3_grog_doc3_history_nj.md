## 2. Fact Plumbing (Explicit Assertions & Unstated Assumptions)

### Explicit Assertions of Fact

#### A. Race to the Top Adoption and the Schundler Dismissal (2010)
- New Jersey State Board of Education -> adopted -> the Common Core State Standards (CCSS) in mathematics and English language arts on June 16, 2010, during the administration of Governor Chris Christie.
- New Jersey Department of Education -> submitted -> an application for $400 million in federal *Race to the Top* (RTTT) Round 2 funding in June 2010.
- United States Department of Education (USDOE) -> scored -> New Jersey's RTTT application at 437.2 points out of 500, placing the state 11th and missing the funding threshold by 3.0 points (0.6% behind Ohio and Massachusetts).
- USDOE Grant Evaluation Panel -> deducted -> 4.8 points from New Jersey's application because Section 8 of the application provided budget expenditure comparisons between FY 2008 and FY 2009 rather than between FY 2009 and FY 2010 as requested in application instructions.
- Governor Chris Christie -> terminated -> New Jersey Education Commissioner Bret Schundler on August 27, 2010, publicly alleging that federal officials engaged in partisan bias and that Schundler misled him regarding the interview presentation.
- US Department of Education -> released -> full unedited video footage of the August 11, 2010 Washington presentation, documenting that federal reviewers asked the New Jersey delegation about the missing 2010 budget figures and that Commissioner Schundler attempted to provide written documentation during the meeting, which reviewers declined per federal protocol.

#### B. The Newark Education Reform Laboratory and Philanthropic Deployment (2010–2015)
- Mark Zuckerberg -> announced -> a $100 million matching gift to Newark Public Schools on *The Oprah Winfrey Show* on September 24, 2010, alongside Governor Chris Christie and Newark Mayor Cory Booker.
- The Foundation for Newark's Future (FNF) -> raised and distributed -> $200 million ($100 million from Zuckerberg and $100 million in philanthropic matching funds) between 2010 and 2015.
- Governor Chris Christie -> appointed -> Christopher Cerf (former NYC Deputy Chancellor under Joel Klein) as New Jersey Commissioner of Education in 2011.
- Commissioner Christopher Cerf -> appointed -> Cami Anderson (former director of Teach For America NYC and NYC DOE alternative superintendent) as State District Superintendent of Newark Public Schools in May 2011.
- Superintendent Cami Anderson -> implemented -> the "One Newark" centralized universal enrollment system in 2014, utilizing an algorithmic matching model to assign students across district and participating public charter schools.
- Superintendent Cami Anderson -> launched -> the "Renew Schools" initiative, closing or restructuring low-performing district schools, replacing school leadership and staff, and leasing or transferring district facilities to charter management networks (including TEAM Academy/KIPP and North Star/Uncommon Schools).
- Newark Public Schools and Newark Teachers Union (NTU) -> ratified -> a landmark collective bargaining agreement in November 2012 that established peer review evaluations and annual merit-pay bonuses of up to $12,500 based on classroom evaluations and Student Growth Percentiles (SGPs).
- Foundation for Newark's Future -> expended -> over $20 million on management consulting firms (including McKinsey & Company, Parthenon Group, and Global Education Advisors) for strategic planning and system reorganization.
- Harvard Center for Education Policy Research (Kane et al., 2017) -> published -> longitudinal evaluation finding that Newark student achievement experienced initial declines from 2011 to 2013 during restructuring, followed by modest net positive ELA gains (+0.07 SD/year) by 2015–2016 that were concentrated almost entirely within expanding charter schools, while neighborhood district schools saw increased enrollment deficits and budgetary friction.

#### C. The PARCC Testing Rollout, Opt-Out Movement, and NJSLS Rebranding (2012–2016)
- New Jersey Legislature -> enacted -> the TEACHNJ Act (P.L. 2012, c. 26; N.J.S.A. 18A:6-117 et seq.) in August 2012, mandating the "AchieveNJ" evaluation system linking 30% to 50% of teacher evaluations in tested grades to Student Growth Percentiles (SGPs) on state standardized tests.
- NJDOE -> transitioned -> from the pencil-and-paper New Jersey Assessment of Knowledge and Skills (NJ ASK) and High School Proficiency Assessment (HSPA) to the computer-based Partnership for Assessment of Readiness for College and Careers (PARCC) exams in Spring 2015.
- Pearson Education -> administered -> the computer-based PARCC assessments across New Jersey school districts under a multi-state consortium contract.
- New Jersey school districts -> reported -> severe implementation obstacles during the Spring 2015 PARCC rollout, including bandwidth constraints, device shortages, software testing lockouts, and extensive testing windows taking up to 10 hours per student across fall/spring performance batteries.
- Suburban parent organizations and Save Our Schools NJ -> organized -> a statewide test refusal ("opt-out") campaign, resulting in over 130,000 New Jersey students (roughly 15% of eligible test-takers statewide, and exceeding 30% in high-wealth suburban districts such as Montclair, Princeton, and Livingston) refusing to take the Spring 2015 PARCC exams.
- Governor Chris Christie -> delivered -> an address at Burlington County College on May 28, 2015, repudiating Common Core ("It is simply not working") and establishing the Study Commission on the Use of Student Assessments.
- New Jersey State Board of Education -> voted -> in May 2016 to replace Common Core with the "New Jersey Student Learning Standards" (NJSLS) and subsequently rebranded PARCC as the New Jersey Student Learning Assessments (NJSLA).
- Curriculum comparison audits -> demonstrated -> that NJSLS retained 84% of Common Core mathematics standards and over 80% of ELA standards verbatim, modifying phrasing in 16% of math standards and adding minor localized guidance while leaving foundational sequence and progression intact.

#### D. Persistent Literacy Deficits and the Science of Reading Restructuring (2020–2026)
- NJDOE NJSLA Assessment Data (2022–2025) -> documented -> persistent achievement gaps in reading proficiency, with only 27.7% of Newark Public Schools 3rd graders and less than 35% of 3rd graders in economically disadvantaged Abbott/SDA districts scoring proficient in ELA.
- State Curriculum Surveys (2023) -> revealed -> that over 65% of New Jersey public school districts utilized "balanced literacy" curricula employing three-cueing strategies (MSV: Meaning, Syntax, Visual) and leveled readers (e.g., Lucy Calkins' Units of Study, Fountas & Pinnell Classroom) permitted under local "municipal home rule" district discretion.
- Governor Mikie Sherrill -> enacted -> an executive and administrative overhaul of NJDOE in early 2026:
  - Transferred Acting Commissioner Kevin Dehmer to Chief Technology Officer.
  - Nominated Dr. Lily Laux (former Deputy Commissioner of School Programs and Standards at the Texas Education Agency) as New Jersey Commissioner of Education.
  - Established the Division of Literacy, Education, and Reading (LEAR) within NJDOE.
  - Promulgated mandatory universal K–3 early literacy screeners (DIBELS 8th Edition, Acadience) administered three times annually across all public and charter LEAs.
  - Established a state High-Quality Instructional Materials (HQIM) list restricting state textbook subsidies to systematic phonics and knowledge-rich structured literacy curricula (e.g., CKLA, Wit & Wisdom, EL Education) while banning three-cueing instruction.
  - Funded high-impact tutoring pilots ($15 million allocation) in urban and rural districts (Camden, Elk Township) that demonstrated a doubling of foundational phonemic proficiency rates within targeted pilot cohorts over a 12-month cycle.

#### E. Comparative Cross-State Jurisdictional Record
- Texas State Board of Education and Texas Legislature -> rejected -> Common Core in 2010 and enacted House Bill 462 in 2013, prohibiting the adoption of CCSS and consortia assessments (PARCC/Smarter Balanced), preserving Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS), and enacting HB 1605 (2023) allocating $324M for state-developed structured literacy curriculum.
- New York State Education Department (NYSED) -> adopted -> CCSS in 2010, created the centralized EngageNY curriculum portal, and experienced a massive suburban opt-out revolt in Spring 2015–2016 where over 20% (200,000+ students) boycotted state assessments, leading to the replacement of CCSS with the NYS Next Generation Learning Standards.
- Massachusetts Board of Elementary and Secondary Education -> voted -> in July 2010 to abandon its 1993 Massachusetts Curriculum Frameworks to adopt Common Core to secure $250M in RTTT funds; voted in November 2015 to exit the PARCC consortium and develop a hybridized Next-Generation MCAS test.

### Unstated Assumptions

1. **The "Standards Mysticism" Assumption:** Proponents assumed that codifying rigorous end-of-year testing targets (the "what") would automatically compel local districts and teachers to discover and implement effective instructional science (the "how"), regardless of widespread district reliance on disproven whole-language cueing mechanisms.
2. **The Structural Fungibility Assumption:** Reform architects assumed that pedagogical and structural models developed in private philanthropic settings could be uniformly deployed across heavily decentralized, municipal "home-rule" public districts without provoking administrative friction or parental opt-out resistance.
3. **The Psychometric Proxy Assumption:** Reformers assumed that Student Growth Percentiles (SGPs) calculated from unpiloted, computer-adaptive PARCC assessments functioned as an objective, statistically reliable metric of individual teacher effectiveness suitable for high-stakes tenure determinations.
4. **The Curricular Rebranding Assumption:** Policymakers assumed that politically renaming Common Core (CCSS $\rightarrow$ NJSLS) and assessment suites (PARCC $\rightarrow$ NJSLA) would placate suburban voter pushback while maintaining 80%+ identical instructional standards without systemic retraining or materials replacement.

## 3. Strict Neutral Summary

The document presents a detailed chronological, political, and empirical account of educational policy, standardized testing, and standards reform in New Jersey from 2010 to 2026. 

The narrative begins with New Jersey's June 2010 administrative adoption of the Common Core State Standards under Governor Chris Christie in an effort to secure $400 million in federal *Race to the Top* Round 2 funding. The application was disqualified after finishing 3.0 points below the funding line due to a clerical budget error, leading to the dismissal of Commissioner Bret Schundler and subsequent disputes regarding federal scoring protocols. Concurrently, Newark Public Schools served as a major laboratory for venture philanthropy following Mark Zuckerberg’s $100 million matching gift, which funded Superintendent Cami Anderson’s "One Newark" algorithmic enrollment plan, charter school expansion, merit-pay teacher contracts, and corporate management consulting engagements. Longitudinal evaluations by Harvard researchers indicated that while charter school cohorts demonstrated ELA gains, traditional district schools experienced severe operational strain and stagnant achievement.

The text documents the implementation of the computerized PARCC testing system in 2014–2015, which intersected with the TEACHNJ Act tying teacher tenure to Student Growth Percentiles. Severe technical glitches and testing burdens triggered a massive suburban opt-out revolt involving over 130,000 students. In response to political pressure, Governor Christie repudiated Common Core in a 2015 address at Burlington County College, leading to the formal rebranding of state benchmarks as the New Jersey Student Learning Standards (NJSLS) and NJSLA tests—though textual audits showed over 80% of standards remained identical to Common Core.

The final sections analyze persistent literacy failures into the 2020s, highlighted by data showing only 27.7% of Newark third graders reading proficiently, driven in part by district reliance on balanced literacy and three-cueing under municipal home-rule discretion. Under Governor Mikie Sherrill in 2026, the state restructured the NJDOE, recruited former Texas Education Agency leadership (Dr. Lily Laux), created the Division of LEAR, mandated universal K–3 screeners, and established structured literacy HQIM mandates, drawing heavily from Texas's non-Common Core curriculum models.
