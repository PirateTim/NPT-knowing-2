## 2. Fact Plumbing (Explicit Assertions & Unstated Assumptions)

### Explicit Assertions of Fact

#### Grassroots Parental Resistance & Friction (2010–2014)
- New Jersey State Board of Education -> adopted -> Common Core State Standards in June 2010.
- Elementary school parents -> experienced -> acute instructional difficulty assisting children with CCSS mathematics homework (multi-step conceptual explanations, ten-frame modeling, non-standard decomposition).
- Implementation of PARCC computerized assessments -> caused -> widespread student test anxiety, diversion of computer labs, and significant classroom hours allocated to standardized test preparation.
- Suburban and rural communities -> perceived -> CCSS and testing consortia as top-down federal curricular mandates that bypassed local school boards and commercialized student psychometric data.

#### The 2014–2015 PARCC Opt-Out Revolt
- Spring 2015 PARCC administration -> triggered -> organized statewide exam refusals led by Save Our Schools NJ (SOSNJ) and the New Jersey Opt-Out Coalition.
- High-performing suburban districts (Montclair, Princeton, Cherry Hill, Livingston) -> recorded -> PARCC refusal rates between 15% and 30%, with specific high schools exceeding 50% refusal among 11th-grade students.
- Opt-out rates -> concentrated disproportionately in -> affluent suburban districts rather than urban state-operated districts (Newark, Camden, Paterson) due to administrative enforcement and graduation sanction risks.
- NJDOE Commissioner David Hespe & Christie Administration -> issued threats -> to withhold state and federal Title I aid from non-compliant districts under NCLB 95% participation mandates.

#### Executive Reversal & The NJSLS Rebranding (2015–2016)
- Governor Chris Christie -> delivered address on May 28, 2015 at Burlington County College -> publicly repudiating Common Core ("Common Core standards have simply not worked... We must scrap the Common Core").
- Christie -> mandated -> NJDOE creation of a Standards Review Committee (parents, teachers, administrators) to rewrite state learning frameworks.
- New Jersey State Board of Education -> formally adopted -> New Jersey Student Learning Standards (NJSLS) in May 2016.
- Independent curriculum audits (Fordham Institute, academic departments) -> established -> NJSLS retained >80% of ELA standards and ~84% of mathematics standards verbatim from original CCSS text.
- State of New Jersey -> continued -> PARCC testing through 2018 before renaming the assessment the New Jersey Student Learning Assessment (NJSLA) while maintaining identical underlying psychometric scales.

#### Longitudinal Sentiment & 2024–2026 Policy Overhaul
- Longitudinal public surveys (Eagleton Polling, FDU PublicMind) -> documented -> persistent parental dissatisfaction regarding foundational literacy and basic computational math fluency from 2016 to 2026.
- Post-pandemic literacy assessments -> revealed -> over 50% of elementary students in non-affluent New Jersey districts were unable to read at grade level despite 15 years of CCSS/NJSLS standards.
- Parental advocacy by 2024 -> transitioned from -> anti-testing opt-out protests to demands for structured phonics and "Science of Reading" instruction.
- Governor Mikie Sherrill Administration -> established -> Division of Literacy, Education, and Reading (LEAR) to publish universal K–3 screening data and provide parental toolkits grounded in cognitive reading science.

### Unstated Assumptions
- Parental comprehension of elementary homework assignments is a necessary prerequisite for sustainable public education reform.
- Standardized test refusal in affluent communities reflects dissatisfaction with pedagogical efficacy rather than an effort to insulate suburban schools from comparative accountability.
- Executive rebranding of controversial educational frameworks without substantial modification of underlying standards is sufficient to placate political opposition while maintaining administrative continuity.
- Transitioning state policy from abstract standards compliance to cognitive reading science (Science of Reading) addresses the structural drivers of historical literacy stagnation.

## 3. Strict Neutral Summary

The document chronicles the political, administrative, and grassroots trajectory of Common Core implementation, backlash, and restructuring in New Jersey between 2010 and 2026. Following state adoption in June 2010, parental resistance expanded significantly between 2011 and 2014, driven by homework alienation surrounding multi-step math decomposition, anxiety over computerized PARCC testing, and concerns regarding local school board autonomy. 

This resistance culminated in the Spring 2015 PARCC Opt-Out movement, during which affluent suburban districts experienced exam refusal rates between 15% and 50% among high school cohorts, prompting administrative threats of Title I funding withholding. In response to mounting suburban political pressure, Governor Chris Christie publicly repudiated Common Core in May 2015, ordering a comprehensive review. In May 2016, the State Board of Education adopted the New Jersey Student Learning Standards (NJSLS); however, subsequent curriculum audits demonstrated that the NJSLS retained over 80% of CCSS ELA standards and 84% of mathematics standards verbatim, while computerized testing continued under the rebranded NJSLA. 

Longitudinal surveys between 2016 and 2026 indicated lingering dissatisfaction with literacy and math fluency, eventually leading to a post-2024 parental movement demanding structured literacy. This shift prompted the Sherrill administration to establish the Division of Literacy, Education, and Reading (LEAR) to implement universal K–3 screening and evidence-based reading instruction.
