### 3. Summary
Dr. Timnit Gebru provides a critical deconstruction of the summer 2026 generative AI hype cycle, examining high-profile announcements by leading foundation model developers, including Anthropic and OpenAI. The article reviews corporate claims that models such as Anthropic’s Claude Mythos have surpassed human experts in cybersecurity and that OpenAI’s Astra has independently solved longstanding open mathematical problems. Gebru contrasts these promotional narratives with independent audits by domain experts: cybersecurity professionals determined that incidents like the Hugging Face security compromise stemmed from basic operational oversights rather than autonomous machine initiative, while mathematicians—led by NYU’s Tristan Buckmaster—identified uncredited intellectual reproduction and research misconduct in OpenAI’s claimed mathematical achievements.

The analysis further investigates why foundation model developers focus capability claims on formal mathematics and software engineering, noting that these fields offer automated symbolic verifiers and compilers that bypass the necessity of paying human subject-matter experts. Gebru details how public figures, including resigning Anthropic engineer Jacob Coxon and lawmakers like Senator Bernie Sanders, focus public concern on speculative, future existential threats of superintelligence. Gebru argues that this existential focus serves to deflect regulatory and public attention away from immediate material externalities: large-scale copyright extraction, severe labor exploitation of Global South data annotators (such as Sama contractors in Kenya), and the heavy local environmental burdens of hyperscale data centers, including electrical grid strain, water depletion, and diesel generator emissions.
