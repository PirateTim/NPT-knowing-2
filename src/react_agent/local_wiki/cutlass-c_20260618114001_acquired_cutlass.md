## Epistemic Classification

This artifact is classified as **Narrative Misdirection**.

## Diagnostic Summary

The asset correctly identifies a legitimate societal problem: the sharp decline in available entry-level jobs for university graduates. However, it performs a significant act of epistemic misdirection by framing this as a new, acute crisis—a "jobpocalypse"—precipitated primarily by the recent advent of Artificial Intelligence.

This narrative is false. The erosion of entry-level career paths and the corporate abandonment of internal training pipelines is a chronic, systemic issue that has been accelerating for several decades due to cost-cutting, offshoring, and a shift towards hiring experienced workers over training new ones.

By attributing this long-term trend to a novel technological cause, the article effectively launders decades of corporate policy decisions, obscuring the true provenance of the problem.

## Key Indicators of Misdirection

1.  **Crisis Framing:** The use of terms like "jobpocalypse" and the focus on a "two, two-and-a-half year" pause creates a sense of a sudden, externally-caused crisis, distracting from the long-term internal decisions made by corporations.
2.  **Technological Scapegoating:** AI is presented as the primary causal agent, allowing the narrative to sidestep more complex and damning explanations related to modern corporate strategy and financialization.
3.  **Introduction of Regressive Solutions:** The most telling indicator is the conclusion, where the discourse shifts to proposing "solutions" from the history books: paid apprenticeships or a return to "a kind of indentured labour." This demonstrates the core function of the misdirection: to create a pretext for introducing radical and regressive changes to labor norms that would otherwise be unacceptable.

## Conclusion

This artifact is a significant piece of evidence for the project. It demonstrates how a real and painful social problem can be reframed through a technologically deterministic lens to obscure corporate responsibility and create a rhetorical opening for the destruction of established labor practices. It is not merely a bad analysis; it is an active production of ignorance about the historical and structural causes of a major economic dislocation.
