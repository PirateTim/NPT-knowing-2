=== EPISTEMIC AUDIT ===

PRIMARY CATEGORIZATION: Bothsiderism (Asymmetric Equivalence)

ANALYSIS:
This asset exemplifies a sophisticated form of bothsiderism by creating a false equivalence between two different categories of argumentation: empirical risk and subjective value. It frames a data-supported claim about public safety ("structural integrity reports," "disaster waiting to happen") as one "side" of a debate, and an emotional, cultural argument ("heart of our town," "memories") as the other.

This is an epistemic failure because it subordinates a quantifiable, critical threat to a non-quantifiable, subjective preference. While cultural value is a valid component of civic discourse, this Op-Ed's framing manufactures a "debate" that paralyzes action and obscures the primacy of the safety imperative. It treats the potential for physical harm as an opinion that can be balanced against sentimentality.

SECONDARY FAILURES:
1.  **Truth Decay:** The argument actively contributes to truth decay by suggesting that data-driven conclusions are merely one "side" of a story, holding no more intrinsic weight than emotional appeals. This weakens the role of evidence in public decision-making.

KEY EXEMPLARS:
-   (Asymmetric Equivalence): "The best solution will be found when the rigorous, data-driven analysis of the engineers is forced to contend with the unquantifiable, but no less real, historical and cultural value..."
-   (Explicit Bothsiderism): "Why We Need to Hear Both Sides of the Aethelburg Bridge Debate" and "We must consider both sides."

LESSON: Bothsiderism is not limited to equating truth with falsehood. A more insidious form is the equating of incommensurable types of claims, such as placing empirical data about physical risk on the same level as subjective cultural or emotional values, thereby manufacturing a paralyzing and fallacious debate.
